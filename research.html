AI-Driven Infrastructure Inspection & BIM Integration

M.Eng. Research — 2025 to Present

AI-Enabled Bridge Damage Inspection Framework

Lab: Construction Management and Infrastructure Systems Laboratory, The University of Tokyo

Supervisor: Associate Professor Pang-jo Chun

My graduate research is developing a three-stage, AI-based framework for automated bridge damage inspection that integrates computer vision with building information modeling. The ultimate objective is a seamless pipeline that takes raw inspection imagery and produces spatially-contextualized, severity-tagged damage records embedded directly within a Digital Twin environment — enabling proactive, data-driven infrastructure management.

The first stage employs YOLO-based instance segmentation models to detect and classify bridge surface defects — principally cracks and corrosion — in steel bridge inspection photographs. These models are trained and fine-tuned on domain-specific datasets to achieve reliable performance under the varied lighting, occlusion, and perspective conditions typical of real-world bridge inspections.

In the second stage, the research investigates pixel-to-metric digital scaling methods to automatically quantify damage severity from detected regions. Converting pixel measurements into real-world dimensions without requiring physical reference targets is a core technical challenge, and solving it is essential for generating actionable engineering assessments from Computer Vision outputs.

The third stage centers on BIM integration. A localization workflow developed in Python and Dynamo maps each detected damage instance to its corresponding structural element within a Revit model, automatically populating BIM parameters with inspection findings. This creates a living, damage-aware record that integrates naturally with existing asset management and maintenance scheduling workflows.

Together, these three stages form a fully automated pipeline — from field photography to BIM-embedded damage report — that has the potential to dramatically reduce inspection time, improve repeatability, and provide infrastructure owners with richer, spatially-explicit condition data than traditional manual methods.

Previous Research

Undergraduate Thesis — KUET, 2018

Influence of Recycled Waste Glass as Fine Aggregate in Brick Aggregate Concrete

As part of my B.Sc. in Building Engineering and Construction Management at KUET, I investigated the rheological and mechanical properties of concrete incorporating recycled waste glass as a partial replacement for fine aggregate. This work examined fresh concrete workability, compressive strength, and related properties across varying replacement ratios, contributing to the growing body of knowledge on sustainable and waste-utilizing construction materials. Findings from this research were presented at the 5th International Conference on Civil Engineering for Sustainable Development (ICCESD 2020).
